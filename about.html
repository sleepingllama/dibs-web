Who's behind this

We built this because we were tired of asking twice.

Dibs is made by two people who each kept having the same kind of argument about chores at home, the quiet kind that doesn't even look like a fight until you're in it. Here's the Tuesday that started it, and who's actually building the fix.

The origin story

Two separate arguments about the dishwasher.

Not big ones, the kind that happens on a random Tuesday, dissolves in twenty minutes, and somehow still leaves a residue. Subir had one at home with his partner. Around the same time, Alok had one at home with his. Different households, same fight: one person had loaded it, the other hadn't noticed anyone had. Nobody had actually done anything wrong, and somehow both people in each house felt like they had.

When they compared notes, that's the pattern they kept finding: chores rarely get missed. They get invisible. Someone remembers the trash goes out Thursday. Someone else remembers to buy bags before they run out. Neither of those things ever lands on a list the other person can see, so the one doing the remembering starts to feel like the only one doing anything, and the one who didn't know has no idea there was anything to know.

They didn't want a chore chart. Chore charts assume the fight is about the chores, and it almost never is. It's about whether the work is visible to both people, not just the one carrying it.

So they built a wheel instead of a list. Every task, claimed out loud, sitting where the whole household can see it. No more "I didn't know that was a thing." No more keeping score in your head. Just: you called it, it's yours, everyone can see whose turn it actually is.

Dibs started as two people fixing their own Tuesdays. Turns out a lot of households have the same one.

Ready to stop keeping score?

Dibs turns who-does-what into who-called-it — the whole household, visible on one wheel, so nobody has to keep track from memory.
